Release notes — 1.0.0

Svelp 1.0.0 is the first complete release of the offline-first paper-survey loop: build, print scanner-ready forms, scan, review, export, and move projects between devices. Everything runs locally in your browser.

What shipped in 1.0.0

Questionnaire building in a visual editor or the declarative Svelp source language, with reusable scales, matrices, consent and signature items, and derived numbering.

Print-exact PDF forms with alignment markers and a QR page identifier on every page, in Comfortable, Compact, and Dense profiles plus presets for academic, clinical, and dense clinical layouts, and respondent batches with live progress and cancellation.

Scanning and recognition that are deterministic and geometry-based: pages are identified by QR code, recovered by their corner markers, and answer bubbles are read by measurement. Uncertain answers route to a review queue instead of being guessed, and corrections survive reprocessing.

Response exports as CSV and JSON with a codebook, per-cell diagnostics, and a full audit trail; project transfer between devices as versioned MAHIM files; and an offline-first PWA that keeps working without a connection.

Known limitations

Physical validation is pending. Recognition has been verified against simulated captures of app-generated pages and by unit-tested synthetic corpora; it has not yet been validated across real printers, pens, papers, and cameras. Accuracy claims for physical scans await that validation.

The scanner reads forms generated by Svelp (or matching Svelp page geometry). It is not a general-purpose form scanner and never reads handwriting or free text automatically.

Assembling one very large batch PDF (on the order of a thousand copies of a hundred-question form) can exceed browser memory on modest devices; generation itself, progress reporting, and cancellation are designed so nothing is lost if a batch is stopped.

All data lives in your browser profile on one device. MAHIM files are the only transfer path, and exports are the only way data leaves the device. Clearing browser storage deletes your data; use exports as backups.

Svelp is a survey tool, not a medical device, and makes no clinical accuracy claims.

Browser support

Svelp 1.0.0 is tested on Chromium-based browsers (desktop and Android-class viewports) with automated end-to-end suites covering 360, 390, and 1440 pixel viewports, offline use, and update recovery. No claims are made for other engines: Firefox and Safari are untested.

The release report with exact test counts is available in the open-source repository under qa/.
